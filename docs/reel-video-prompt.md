# Aangan — Instagram reel video prompts

Written 27 August 2026. Pairs with the 15-second hook in `song-prompt.md`.

## Read this before you generate anything

Your whole pitch to owners and students is that **the photographs are real** —
you take them yourself, including the bathroom, and the listing carries a dated
verification record. That is the product.

So do not generate a fake hostel room. If a student recognises an AI room in
your reel, every real photograph on the site gets doubted with it. That is a
cheap thing to lose and an expensive thing to win back.

**Use AI video for the feeling, use your camera for the rooms.**

- AI: the bus window, the road, the city waking up, hands, feet, light through
  a doorway, evening at the lake — shots with no room and no face that has to be
  a real person.
- Your phone: every interior. The room, the bed, the mess plate, the corridor,
  the gate. Shoot these on your listing visits — you are already in the building
  with a camera.

The prompts below are written that way. Shots marked **[SHOOT REAL]** are yours.

---

## How these tools work, so the prompts fit

Most AI video tools give you about **8 seconds per clip**. A 15-second reel is
therefore three or four clips cut together, not one prompt. Each block below is
one clip. Generate them separately and assemble in your editor.

Rules baked into every prompt:

- **Vertical 9:16**, framed for a phone.
- **No text, no signboards, no logos in frame.** AI tools render Devanagari as
  nonsense, and a garbled Marathi sign in a Kolhapur ad is the one mistake
  people will screenshot. Your on-screen words go on in the editor.
- **No faces in close-up** unless it is real footage of a real person who said
  yes. Hands, backs, silhouettes and mid-shots carry the emotion fine.
- Warm late-afternoon light throughout. It matches the song and it is what
  Kolhapur actually looks like at 5pm.

## Reel A — 15 seconds, four clips

### Clip 1 — 0:00 to 0:03 — the cold open

```
Vertical 9:16, cinematic, shot on 35mm lens, shallow depth of field.

A young person's hand rests on the open window frame of a moving state
transport bus in rural Maharashtra, fingers in the wind. Through the window,
green sugarcane fields and red-earth roads slide past in the warm light of late
afternoon. Dust hangs in the sunbeam inside the bus.

Camera: locked off, slight handheld vibration from the moving bus.
Motion: only the landscape moving past and the wind in the fingers.
Colour: warm gold, deep green, red earth. Slight film grain.
No text, no signage, no faces, no logos.
```

### Clip 2 — 0:03 to 0:06 — arriving

```
Vertical 9:16, cinematic, handheld, 28mm lens.

Low angle from behind: a student walks away from camera down a narrow Indian
small-city street carrying a duffel bag over one shoulder and a backpack.
Old two-storey buildings with balconies on both sides, parked scooters, a
chai stall's steam, evening light raking down the street. The person is a
silhouette against the light — face never visible.

Camera: follows behind at walking pace, gentle handheld sway.
Colour: warm amber light, cool blue shadows.
No text, no signage, no readable shop boards, no logos.
```

### Clip 3 — 0:06 to 0:09 — the door **[SHOOT REAL]**

Your own footage. Stand in the corridor of a hostel you are listing. Film a
door being pushed open from the corridor side, the room's light spilling out,
a bag being set down on a bed. Ten seconds is plenty; you will use three.

Shoot it with the window behind you, not in front, so the room is lit and not
a silhouette. Hold the phone vertical and keep it still — walk the phone
forward slowly rather than zooming.

If you have no room footage yet, use this AI shot instead and replace it the
moment you do:

```
Vertical 9:16, cinematic, 35mm.

A simple bare room in an Indian hostel, warm afternoon sunlight falling through
an open window onto an empty bed with a folded blanket. A duffel bag sits on
the floor, just set down. Dust drifts in the light. Nobody in frame.

Camera: slow push in toward the window.
Colour: warm, soft, honest. Not styled, not luxurious. Lived-in and clean.
No text, no logos, no people.
```

### Clip 4 — 0:09 to 0:15 — belonging

```
Vertical 9:16, cinematic, 50mm, shallow depth of field.

Four young students sit on the steps of a building at golden hour, laughing,
shoulders touching, sharing food from steel plates. Shot from behind and
slightly to the side — backs and shoulders, no faces to camera. Behind them,
the warm blur of an Indian small city in the evening.

Camera: slow drift to the right, handheld, alive.
Colour: gold, warm skin tones, soft haze.
Mood: this is home now.
No text, no logos, no direct faces.
```

**Editor's cut:** Clip 1 for 3 seconds, Clip 2 for 3, Clip 3 for 3, Clip 4 for 6.
Cut every one of them on a clap in the song. The last frame should be warm and
still, so the loop back to the bus window feels like a memory, not a jump.

## Fixing bad takes

- **Looks like a stock advertisement**: add *"documentary, unpolished, real,
  imperfect framing, natural light only, no colour grading"*.
- **Wrong country**: say *"western Maharashtra, India, small city"*, and name
  concrete things — sugarcane, red earth, steel plates, scooters. Avoid "Indian"
  alone; it pulls Rajasthan and Kerala clichés.
- **Faces look wrong**: reframe the prompt to backs, hands, silhouettes and
  feet. AI faces are still the giveaway.
- **Garbled text appears**: add *"no text, no signage, no writing, no logos"*
  and generate again. Do not try to fix it in the edit.
- **Too glossy or too rich**: say *"modest, simple, ordinary, working-class,
  clean but not new"*. A luxury room in the reel and a real ₹2,500 room on the
  site is a promise you did not mean to make.

## Before you post

- Your caption does the selling. The video does the feeling, the song says
  अंगण once, the caption says what Aangan is and where to find it.
- If any real person's face is recognisable, get their yes first — in writing
  on WhatsApp is enough. Students are minors sometimes; if they are, ask the
  parent.
- Check what the video tool's terms let you do with the output commercially
  before it goes on a business account.

# The single full-length prompt (Google Flow / Veo)

Flow can run past the eight-second limit by extending a shot in Scenebuilder,
so you can drive the whole thing from one master prompt and then push it
forward. Everything below is written for that.

## Three things Flow does that change the prompt

1. **Veo generates its own audio.** Left alone it will invent dialogue and
   background music that fights your song. The prompt below shuts that off. Mute
   the clip in your editor anyway and lay the song underneath.
2. **Consistency comes from repetition.** When you extend a shot, describe the
   person with the *exact same words* every time — same age, same shirt, same
   bag. Change one adjective and Flow gives you a different human. The
   description is written once below and repeated verbatim in every extension.
3. **Vertical must be set in Flow's output settings**, not only asked for in the
   prompt. Set 9:16 before you generate.

## Master prompt — the full 30 seconds

```
A 30-second continuous cinematic sequence, vertical 9:16, shot on 35mm film,
shallow depth of field, warm late-afternoon light throughout, fine film grain,
documentary feel rather than advertising polish.

THE SUBJECT, identical in every shot: a 19-year-old Indian student, slim, short
black hair, plain dark blue half-sleeve shirt, faded jeans, worn brown canvas
duffel bag over his right shoulder. Seen mostly from behind, in profile, or as
a silhouette. Never a direct close-up of his face.

SETTING: a small city in western Maharashtra, India. Red earth, sugarcane
fields on the outskirts, old two-storey buildings with iron balconies, parked
scooters, steel plates, a large lake at the edge of town. Modest and ordinary,
clean but not new. Never luxurious.

THE SEQUENCE:

0-5s: His hand rests on the open window frame of a moving state transport bus,
fingers in the wind. Through the window, green sugarcane and red-earth roads
slide past. Dust hangs in the sunbeam inside the bus. Camera locked off with
the natural vibration of the moving bus.

5-10s: He steps down from the bus into a narrow street and walks away from
camera, duffel bag on his shoulder, past chai-stall steam and parked scooters,
a silhouette against the low evening light between the buildings. Camera
follows behind him at walking pace, gentle handheld sway.

10-14s: He climbs a narrow concrete staircase inside an old building, one hand
on the iron railing, light falling from a window on the landing above. Camera
below him, tilting up as he climbs.

14-19s: He pushes open a plain wooden door. Warm sunlight floods out of a
simple room onto the corridor floor. He sets the duffel bag down on a bare bed
with a folded blanket, sits on the edge of the bed, and looks toward the open
window. Camera pushes in slowly from the corridor, through the doorway.

19-25s: Golden hour. He sits on stone steps outside with three other students,
all of them laughing, shoulders touching, eating from steel plates. Shot from
behind and slightly to the side. Camera drifts slowly right, handheld, alive.

25-30s: Wide and still. The group of them small in the frame, silhouettes on
the promenade beside a large calm lake at dusk, hills in the distance, the last
orange light going out of the sky. He is in the middle of them now. Camera
static, very slow push in. The shot holds on stillness.

COLOUR: warm gold and amber highlights, deep green, red earth, cool blue
shadows. Soft haze at golden hour.

MOOD ARC: leaving, alone, arriving, belonging.

AUDIO: no dialogue, no speech, no singing, no background music, no soundtrack.
Ambient sound only — wind, distant traffic, birds. Silence is fine.

DO NOT INCLUDE: any text, any writing, any signage, any shop boards, any
logos, any watermark. No direct close-ups of faces. No luxury interiors, no
modern apartment, no high-rise, no western city.
```

# All-in-one: let Flow write the song, sing it, and shoot it

One prompt, one output — lyrics, melody, vocal and picture together.

## What to expect honestly

Flow's video model sings, but it is far stronger in English than in Marathi.
Devanagari lyrics often come back as sounds that are nearly Marathi and not
quite — which a Kolhapur student hears instantly. Two consequences:

- **Listen to the words before you post anything.** If a line is mush, it is
  mush to your audience too.
- **Keep one word non-negotiable: अंगण / Aangan.** Everything else Flow may
  invent. If it garbles the brand word, regenerate — that is the only line the
  whole video exists to deliver.

If the sung Marathi keeps coming out wrong after a few tries, use Prompt C
below: Flow sings a mostly-English lyric and only chants the Marathi hook, which
it handles far better.

## Prompt A — Flow writes everything (start here)

```
Create a 30-second vertical 9:16 music video with an original song, sung
performance and picture together.

THE SONG — write the lyrics yourself, to this brief:
Language: Marathi, with the single word "अंगण" (aangan) as the hook, sung
clearly and repeated. Aangan means the courtyard of a family home.
Subject: a 19-year-old leaving his village for the first time to study in a
small city, missing home, and slowly finding a new one among friends.
Feeling: nostalgic at the start, warm and belonging by the end. Hopeful, never
sad, never tragic.
Do not name any brand, company, website or product. Do not advertise anything.
The word "अंगण" carries the whole meaning on its own.

THE MUSIC:
Warm Marathi acoustic folk-pop. 88 BPM, gentle 6/8 sway.
Fingerpicked acoustic guitar as the spine, dholki entering at the chorus,
harmonium pads underneath, bansuri answering the vocal between lines, hand
claps and a small group of voices on the final chorus.
No EDM, no heavy bass, no electric guitar, no autotune, no orchestral swell.
One warm young male lead voice, chest voice, slightly imperfect and human, like
a recording made in a small room. A soft female harmony on the last chorus only.

THE PICTURE — cinematic, shot on 35mm film, shallow depth of field, warm
late-afternoon light throughout, fine film grain, documentary feel rather than
advertising polish.

THE SUBJECT, identical in every shot: a 19-year-old Indian student, slim, short
black hair, plain dark blue half-sleeve shirt, faded jeans, worn brown canvas
duffel bag over his right shoulder. Seen mostly from behind, in profile, or as
a silhouette. Never a direct close-up of his face. He does not lip-sync — the
song plays over the picture, he simply lives inside it.

SETTING: a small city in western Maharashtra, India. Red earth, sugarcane
fields on the outskirts, old two-storey buildings with iron balconies, parked
scooters, steel plates, a large lake at the edge of town. Modest and ordinary,
clean but not new. Never luxurious.

THE SEQUENCE, cut to the song:

0-5s, under the opening line: his hand on the open window frame of a moving
state transport bus, fingers in the wind, sugarcane and red-earth roads sliding
past, dust in the sunbeam. Camera locked off, natural bus vibration.

5-10s, as the loneliness builds: he steps down from the bus and walks away from
camera down a narrow street past chai-stall steam and parked scooters, a
silhouette against low evening light. Camera follows behind at walking pace,
handheld.

10-14s, last quiet moment: he climbs a narrow concrete staircase inside an old
building, hand on the iron railing, light falling from a landing window above.
Camera below, tilting up.

14-19s, ON THE FIRST "अंगण" OF THE CHORUS: he pushes open a plain wooden door
and warm sunlight floods out of a simple room onto the corridor floor. He sets
the bag down on a bare bed and sits, looking toward the open window. Camera
pushes slowly through the doorway. This cut must land exactly on the word.

19-25s, chorus in full: golden hour, he sits on stone steps with three other
students, all laughing, shoulders touching, eating from steel plates, shot from
behind and slightly to the side. Camera drifts right, handheld, alive.

25-30s, final line: wide and still, the group small in the frame, silhouettes
on the promenade beside a large calm lake at dusk, hills in the distance, the
last orange light going. He is in the middle of them now. Camera static, very
slow push in, holding on stillness.

COLOUR: warm gold and amber, deep green, red earth, cool blue shadows, soft
haze at golden hour.

MOOD ARC: leaving, alone, arriving, belonging.

DO NOT INCLUDE: any text, any writing, any signage, any shop boards, any
subtitles, any logos, any watermark. No spoken dialogue — singing only. No
direct close-ups of faces. No lip-syncing to camera. No luxury interiors, no
modern apartment, no high-rise, no western city.
```

## Prompt B — same video, but the lyrics locked

THE SONG — sing exactly these Marathi lyrics, no others, no added lines:

[Verse]

अंगण! अंगण!

## Prompt C — English lyric, Marathi hook only

The safety version, if sung Marathi keeps failing. Same picture, same music.
Song brief becomes:

```
THE SONG — write the lyrics yourself in simple English, about a 19-year-old
leaving his village for the first time to study in a small Indian city, missing
home, and finding a new one among friends. Hopeful, never sad.

The chorus hook is the Marathi word "अंगण", pronounced AHN-gun, chanted and
repeated by a small group of voices, and then this English line:
"Far from home and still like home."

Do not name any brand, company, website or product.
```

## Checking the output before it goes anywhere

- Play it with the sound on and confirm **अंगण** is a clean, recognisable word.
- Confirm no invented text or signage crept into the picture.
- Confirm the lyric did not invent a claim — no promises about rooms, prices or
  a service. The song must not sell; the caption sells.
- Have a Kolhapur Marathi speaker listen once. A line that sounds translated
  will be heard as translated.
- Check Flow's terms on commercial use before it goes on a business account.
