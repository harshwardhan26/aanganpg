# Owner meetings — what to know before you walk in

Written 19 August 2026, for meetings starting 20 August.

---

## 1. Where the product actually stands

Be clear-eyed about this before you're standing in someone's doorway.

**What exists and works:**
- The site is built and runs. Every page renders on a phone.
- 15 Kolhapur colleges are in the system, each with its own landing page.
- The listing page is genuinely good — photos, a dated and named verification
  record, and a "short answers" block covering warden, gate closing time,
  deposit, beds free, food, and walking distance to the college.
- The publish rules are enforced by the system, not by memory: six photos
  minimum, a bathroom photo compulsory, a thali photo if a mess is claimed,
  and warden name plus gate time on every girls' listing.

**What does not exist yet:**
- **Zero listings.** The database has no properties in it. Search returns
  nothing. The homepage shows a "photographs coming this week" placeholder.
- The site is not deployed to a public domain yet.
- A listing cannot currently be completed from a phone (a known bug — photo
  tagging needs a mouse). **Assume you enter listings at a laptop in the
  evening, not standing in the PG.**
- Nothing notifies you when a student enquiry arrives. You have to open the
  admin page and look.

**What this means for the pitch:** you are not selling traffic. You have none.
You are selling free professional photographs, a free listing, and a fee that
only exists if a student actually moves in. That is a genuinely good offer and
it costs the owner nothing to say yes to. Lead with that.

**Never say** the site already has students on it, or quote a number of visitors.
Kolhapur PG owners talk to each other. One inflated claim, checked once, and you
are finished in this market.

---

## 2. The one line

> "आम्ही तुमची रूम फुकट फोटो काढून साइटवर टाकतो. विद्यार्थी आला आणि रूम भरली,
> तरच पैसे. नाहीतर काहीच नाही."

Everything else in the meeting is elaboration on that sentence.

---

## 3. The flow, from the owner's side

1. You visit. You look at the rooms, meet the owner, note the rent, deposit,
   facilities, rules, gate timing, warden.
2. You photograph everything yourself — including the bathroom.
3. Owner signs the listing agreement. One page.
4. You put the listing up. Free. The owner's own number is on it.
5. A student finds it, contacts Aangan, and you arrange the visit.
6. **You go with the student to the PG.** Owner, student, and you in the room.
7. Student and owner agree the rent between themselves. You do not set it.
8. Room confirmed → owner pays the one-time fee that day → everyone signs the
   short slip.
9. Nothing after that, ever. No monthly cut, no share of renewals.

Step 6 is the part owners will ask about. Frame it as service, not supervision:
you are bringing a student who is serious, who has already seen the photographs,
who knows the rent, and who is not going to waste their afternoon.

---

## 4. The money — know this arithmetic cold

Take a ₹5,000 bed.

- Empty, it earns **nothing**, every month, until it is filled.
- One month empty = **₹5,000 gone**. It is not recoverable.
- Your fee at half of the first month = **₹2,500**, paid once.
- That is **fifteen days of vacancy**. If you fill the bed even two weeks faster
  than the owner would have alone, the fee has already paid for itself.
- If the student stays the academic year — say 11 months — the owner earns
  **₹55,000** on that bed and paid **4.5% of it**, once, to fill it.

**The sentence to use:**

> "तुमची खोली पंधरा दिवस रिकामी राहिली तरी तेवढाच तोटा होतो. आमची फी तेवढीच आहे —
> आणि ती फक्त एकदाच."

**The fee amount is not finally decided.** Do not commit to a number in the first
few meetings if you can avoid it. Instead ask — see section 8.

---

## 5. What you are asking the owner to agree to

Five things. Know them without looking.

1. Photographs — you take them, you own them, they go on the site.
2. The rent, deposit, and facilities you write down are correct today, and the
   owner tells you when they change.
3. When an Aangan student visits, an Aangan person comes along.
4. Answer a student's call or message within about an hour.
5. The one-time fee, paid on the day the room is confirmed.

Numbers 1 and 3 are the ones that get pushback. Prepare for those two.

---

## 6. The bathroom photo — your hardest ask, and your whole business

Owners will resist this. It is the shot everybody hides, which is exactly why it
works.

Do not apologise for it or treat it as a technicality. Explain the reason:

> "विद्यार्थी आणि त्यांचे आई-वडील सगळ्यात आधी हाच प्रश्न विचारतात — बाथरूम कसा आहे?
> फोटो नसेल तर ते फोन करत नाहीत, ते पुढच्या रूमला जातात. स्वच्छ बाथरूमचा फोटो
> तुमच्या फायद्याचा आहे — तुमची रूम इतरांपेक्षा वेगळी दिसते."

If the bathroom genuinely is not presentable, that is useful information. Say you
will come back after it is cleaned. Do not list it without the photo — the system
will not let you publish anyway, and the rule is the reason a student trusts the
listing at all.

## 7. Objections, and what to say

**"माझ्या रूम्स भरतातच. गेटवर बोर्ड लावला की झालं."**
Fine — then this costs you nothing. Free photos, free listing, and if it never
sends you a student you never pay a rupee. Ask: has any bed been empty for more
than a month in the last year? Almost always the answer is yes.

**"ब्रोकर माझ्याकडून पैसे घेत नाही, तो विद्यार्थ्याकडून घेतो."**
Exactly — and that is why the student does not trust him. We take nothing from
the student, so the student trusts the listing and the rent stays what you
actually asked for. You pay only when the bed is filled.

**"तुमच्या साइटवर तर काहीच नाही."**
True. Say so. "आम्ही आत्ता सुरुवात करतोय. म्हणूनच फोटो आणि लिस्टिंग फुकट आहे."
Never bluff on this one — it is checkable in ten seconds.

**"अर्धा महिना जास्त होतंय."**
Fifteen days of vacancy costs the same. And it is once, not monthly. Then ask
what they think is fair — see section 8.

**"विद्यार्थी एका महिन्यात सोडून गेला तर?"**
Be honest: the fee is for filling the bed, not for guaranteeing the tenancy. But
note that you are bringing a student who has already seen accurate photographs,
knows the real rent and the real rules, and has visited before deciding — which
is precisely why they are less likely to leave than a walk-in.

**"मी विद्यार्थ्याला डायरेक्ट घेतलं तर?"**
Do not get defensive. Say plainly that this is why you come to the visit and why
the slip gets signed the same day. Then say the real thing: you will keep sending
students all year, and it costs nothing to keep that going.

**"माझा नंबर साइटवर टाकणार? स्पॅम येईल."**
Yes, your number is on the listing — that is the point, students call you
directly and there is no middleman on the phone. Only people looking at your
actual room see it.

**"तुम्ही कोण? कुठली कंपनी?"**
Have a straight answer ready: your name, that you are from Kolhapur, that you
visit and photograph every property yourself. Show the site on your phone.
Show `/verification` — the page that says plainly what you do *not* check. That
page will do more for your credibility than any claim you can make out loud.

---

## 8. Ask this in every single meeting

> "एक बेड भरून मिळाला, तर तुम्हाला त्याची किंमत किती वाटते?"

Get five answers before you fix your fee. If owners say ₹1,000, your model still
works at ₹1,000. If they say "I have never paid anyone anything" — that is the
single most important thing you can learn this week, and it is worth more than
closing any one of these meetings.

Also worth asking:
- How many beds do you have, and how many are empty right now?
- How long does a bed usually stay empty?
- How do students find you today?
- Do you pay anyone at present — a broker, a senior student, a canteen owner?

Write the answers down the same day.

---

## 9. What to carry

- Phone, charged, with the site open and the `/verification` page ready.
- A laptop or the ability to enter the listing properly the same evening.
- Both documents printed, several copies: the one-page agreement and the closing
  slip. **These are not built yet** — get them printed before you go.
- A pen that works.
- Good light for photographs. Mornings are better than afternoons.

---

## 10. Season — say this out loud to yourself before you start

It is late August. The main admission rush is largely over for this year. Most
students who needed a room in June and July already have one.

That is not a reason to wait. It is a reason to change the pitch:

> "आत्ता सिझन संपत आलाय, म्हणूनच आत्ता फोटो काढून ठेवू. पुढच्या वर्षी जून-जुलैला
> तुमची रूम तयार असेल — आणि तोपर्यंत मधल्या काळात कोणी विद्यार्थी आला तर तो
> तुमच्याकडेच येईल."

Signing owners now and photographing now means you enter next June with a full
catalogue instead of an empty site. That is the real goal of this month's
meetings — not revenue.

---

## 11. What never to promise

- A number of students, or any timeline for filling a bed.
- Anything about a student's behaviour, payment, or how long they stay.
- Anything about safety or security — `/verification` says explicitly that Aangan
  gives no safety guarantee. Do not contradict your own site in a doorway.
- That the listing will appear at the top of anything.
- A fee figure you have not decided. "मी नक्की सांगून कळवतो" is a complete answer.

---

## 12. After every meeting, the same day

- Photographs uploaded and the listing entered while you still remember the
  details.
- Owner's name, number, bed count, and current vacancies written down.
- Their answer to the "what is a filled bed worth" question written down.
- Signed agreement filed — photograph it with your phone as a backup.

Ten signed owners with complete, honest listings is a real business by next June.
Fifty half-finished ones is not.
