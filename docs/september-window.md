# The September window — calendar, field plan, and the pitch that uses it

Written 19 August 2026. Supersedes section 10 ("Season") of `owner-pitch-brief.md`.

Source: MHT-CET FE 2026 official schedule, `fe2026.mahacet.org` → Important Dates.
Verify the page again on 24 Aug and 3 Sep — the state has moved these dates in
past years, sometimes by two or three days. If a date here disagrees with the
site, the site is right.

### What this means in plain terms

A student learns which city they are moving to on the **allotment date**, and has
to physically stand in that city within **three to four days**. That gap is the
entire room-hunting window, and it is the only time the buyer, the parent, the
money and the unmet need are all in Kolhapur at once.

There are three such gaps this year. Roughly sixteen days total.

After 15 September, no new first-year is admitted for this academic year. Room
demand does not literally fall to zero — hostel-leavers, transfers, and students
unhappy with a panic-booked room still move — but the fresher wave is finished.

## 2. The three waves, and what to do in each

### Wave 1 — 25 to 28 August (four days, starts in six days)

You will not have a catalogue. Do not try to build one for this.

Go with a phone, a notebook, and three to five rooms you have personally walked
through. Match by hand.

Goals, in this order:

1. **Listen.** Write down the first three questions every parent asks. That is
   your real product spec, and you cannot get it any other way.
2. **Collect numbers.** Every student who talks to you, name and phone, plus
   which college and what rent they had in mind.
3. **Close two.** Anything above zero is a win at this stage.

Position: not a website. A person from Kolhapur who knows the PGs near the
college and will show them for free. That is completely true and needs no
product behind it.

### Wave 2 — 4 to 7 September (four days)

You have two weeks between now and this. Target: **12 to 15 photographed
listings within walking distance of the college you are targeting.**

This is the wave where the site becomes useful. Show it on your phone at the
gate — not as a marketplace, as a photo album of rooms you can walk them to
this afternoon.

### Wave 3 — 8 to 15 September (institute-level round)

Your best shot. These students are the least prepared of all: they are admitted
directly by the college, often days before the cut-off, frequently after being
rejected elsewhere. Nobody plans a room for a seat they did not expect to get.

By now you have three weeks of learning, a real catalogue, and a script that has
survived two waves.

### After 15 September

Freshers market is closed. Everything after that — including any college event —
is list-building for the November–January switchers and next June's cycle.
Collect numbers, promise nothing, spend no money.

---

## 3. What to carry to a wave day

- Phone charged, plus a power bank. You will be out six hours.
- Printed photographs of your listed rooms. Paper works when the network does not,
  and a parent will hold a photo longer than they will hold your phone.
- A notebook. Numbers on paper do not get lost in a phone that dies.
- The rate card in your head, not on a flyer.
- Water, and something to sit on. This is a standing job.
- Your own ID, visible. Parents will ask who you are before they ask anything else.

**Do not** hand out anything with a URL on it until the catalogue is real.
A WhatsApp number on a small card is enough:
*"जून मध्ये किंवा आत्ता रूम शोधताय? हा नंबर सेव्ह करा."*

## 4. The owner pitch, rebuilt around the deadline

The old pitch asked the owner to believe in a future marketplace. This one asks
him to believe a date published by the state government, which he already
believes, because he watches this cycle every year.

Open with his loss, not with your product.

### The core sequence

**Step 1 — the deadline, before anything else.**

> "काका, एक मिनिट. पंधरा सप्टेंबरनंतर या वर्षी एकही नवीन विद्यार्थी अ‍ॅडमिशन घेणार नाही.
> हे CET च्या वेबसाइटवर आहे, बघा —"

Show the schedule on your phone. Do not explain it. Let him read the 15/09 line.

**Step 2 — ask his number. Do not guess it.**

> "तुमच्याकडे आज किती बेड रिकामे आहेत?"

Wait for the answer. Write it down in front of him. This one question does more
than any sentence you can say — and across twenty owners it gives you the size
of the September market, which nobody else in Kolhapur has.

**Step 3 — do the arithmetic with his number, out loud.**

Say it slowly. Let him do the multiplication himself.

> "तीन बेड रिकामे. एक बेड पाच हजारांचा. पंधरा सप्टेंबरपर्यंत भरले नाहीत, तर पुढचे
> दहा महिने तसेच राहतील. तीन बेड × पाच हजार × दहा महिने — दीड लाख रुपये.
> या वर्षी परत मिळणार नाहीत."

**Step 4 — your offer, in two sentences.**

> "मी फोटो काढतो, माहिती लिहितो, विद्यार्थ्याला इथपर्यंत घेऊन येतो. याचे तुम्ही आज
> काहीही देत नाही.
> विद्यार्थी राहायला लागला, तरच पहिल्या महिन्याच्या भाड्याचे निम्मे — एकदाच.
> विद्यार्थी आला नाही, तर एक रुपयाही नाही."

**Step 5 — the close, which is small on purpose.**

> "आज फक्त फोटो काढून जाऊ का? पैशाचं नंतर बघू."

You are not asking him to sign. You are asking for twenty minutes and some
photographs. That is a much easier yes, it is genuinely reversible, and once his
rooms are photographed he is far more likely to sign.

## 5. The arithmetic card — know this without thinking

For a ₹5,000 bed:

- Empty from now to next June: **₹50,000 lost, permanently.**
- Aangan's fee if filled: **₹2,500, once, after the student moves in.**
- That is **5%** of what the bed earns — paid only out of money he would not
  otherwise have had.
- Put another way: the fee equals **fifteen days of that bed's rent.** The bed
  has already been empty longer than that.

If the owner names a different rent, redo it with his number on the spot. Doing
the multiplication in front of him is more persuasive than any figure you
arrive with.

## 6. Objections this pitch will actually get

**"माझ्या रूम्स भरतातच, मला गरज नाही."**
> "छान. मग आजही रिकाम्या आहेत का? … किती?"
Then go back to step 3 with the number he just gave you. If he genuinely has
zero vacancies, thank him, take his number, and ask to come back in May. Do not
argue with a full house.

**"तुझी साइट तर रिकामी आहे."**
Agree immediately. Never defend it.
> "हो, नवीन आहे. म्हणूनच फोटोचे पैसे घेत नाही. सुरुवात कुठून तरी करावी लागते —
> आणि तुम्ही आज काहीही गमावत नाही."

**"विद्यार्थी आला हे कसं कळणार? तू खोटं सांगशील."**
> "विद्यार्थी माझ्यासोबतच येईल. मी स्वतः असेन. तुम्ही, विद्यार्थी आणि मी — तिघे
> समोरासमोर. नंतर काहीही सांगायची गरज नाही."

**"दोन महिन्यांनी विद्यार्थी सोडून गेला तर?"**
> "तुमचा पहिला महिना मिळालेला असतो, तो परत मागत नाही. आणि तीच रूम मी परत
> लावून देतो — त्याचे वेगळे पैसे नाहीत."
Only promise this if you will honour it. It is a strong line and it costs you
little, because a re-list of a room you have already photographed is cheap.

**"पैसे आधी दे."**
> "नाही. विद्यार्थी राहायला लागल्यावरच. आधी पैसे घेणारा माणूस नंतर दिसत नाही —
> मला इथेच राहायचंय."

**"बाथरूमचा फोटो कशाला?"**
> "मुलगी असेल तर आई पहिल्यांदा तेच विचारते. फोटो नसेल तर ती फोन करत नाही,
> दुसरीकडे जाते. तुमची रूम चांगली आहे — मग लपवायचं काय आहे?"

**"तू कोण, कुठला?"**
Answer plainly — name, where you're from, that you are local. Then show
`/verification` on your phone. The page that lists what you *don't* check will
do more for your credibility than anything you claim out loud.

## 7. Adjusting for age

The full tone guidance is in `owner-pitch-marathi.md`. Keep those registers —
only the opening and the arithmetic change:

- **25–40:** run the whole sequence at speed. Phone out at step 1. He will read
  the schedule himself and reach the conclusion before you say it. Close hard:
  "आज फोटो काढू का?"
- **42–50:** this pitch is built for him. He thinks in vacancy and months. Spend
  the most time on step 2 and step 3 and let the numbers carry it.
- **52–70:** do not open with the deadline — it reads as pressure. Introduce
  yourself first, sit down, then bring the schedule in as information, not as a
  threat: *"तुम्हाला माहीत असेलच, पण नुकतंच बघितलं…"* Slow the arithmetic down.
  Close with no ask at all: *"आज काहीही ठरवायची गरज नाही. फोटो काढून ठेवतो, तुम्ही
  विचार करा."*

---

## 8. What not to say, this month especially

- No student numbers, no traffic claims, no "many students are searching".
  You do not have them and the owner will find out.
- No safety or verification guarantee. `/verification` says explicitly that
  Aangan gives none. Do not contradict your own site in a doorway.
- No promise about *when* a bed fills. The deadline is the state's; the outcome
  is not yours to promise.
- No fee figure you have not finally decided. "मी नक्की सांगून कळवतो" is a
  complete answer.

## 9. Daily rhythm, now until 15 September

**Morning:** owner meetings. Good light for photographs, and owners are free
before the day starts.

**Wave days (25–28 Aug, 4–7 Sep, 8–15 Sep):** at the college instead. Owners can
wait; arriving students cannot.

**Evening, every day without exception:** enter the listings at a laptop.
Photo tagging does not work on a phone yet, so a listing entered on the road
cannot be published. Same evening, while you still remember which bathroom
belonged to which house.

**Written down the same day:** owner name, number, bed count, vacancies today,
and what he said a filled bed is worth to him.

Ten complete listings and forty owner phone numbers by 15 September is a real
position going into next June. Fifty half-entered ones is not.
