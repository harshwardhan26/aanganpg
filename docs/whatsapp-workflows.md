# WhatsApp workflows

Written 20 August 2026. Operational, not code — the code side is Phase F in
`fix-prompt-direct-model.md`.

All message templates are in plain English, kept deliberately simple. Students
arriving through CAP rounds come from all over Maharashtra and English is the
one language every one of them reads.

WhatsApp is where the business actually runs. Call goes to the owner; WhatsApp
comes to you. That makes your inbox the only place you see demand, and the only
place a lead becomes a record.

---

## 0. Set up WhatsApp Business first — 20 minutes, once

The free WhatsApp Business app, not regular WhatsApp. Everything below depends
on it.

**Greeting message** (fires automatically on a first-time message):

> Hello! Welcome to Aangan.
> We got your message. We will reply soon.
> Aangan is free for students. We never ask for booking money, advance or deposit.

The third line is not marketing. It is the sentence that protects a student from
anyone impersonating you later. Keep it in the auto-reply forever.

**Away message** for the hours you cannot answer. Honest and specific:

> We cannot reply right now. We will reply after 8 AM.

**Business profile:** name, Kolhapur, the site URL once the domain is live,
hours you actually keep. Nothing you cannot honour.

**Quick replies** — save these as `/` shortcuts. They are what make a 30-second
reply possible while standing on a road:
`/owner` (owner number + what to ask), `/full` (room is taken), `/free`
(students pay nothing), `/who` (who Aangan is).

**Labels** — this is your lead dashboard until the real one ships:
`New`, `Number given`, `Visited`, `Moved in`, `Did not go`, `June list`.
Label every chat the moment you reply. Two seconds each, and it is the only
record you will have of what happened in September.

## 1. Student enquiry — the main flow

**Trigger:** a student taps WhatsApp on a listing. The message arrives already
carrying the PG name, rent, locality and the listing link, so you know exactly
which room before you read a word.

**Your reply, within 30 minutes during the waves.** Speed is the entire product
here — they are standing on a road with a bag.

> Hi! This PG is still available. ✅
>
> Owner's number: 98XXXXXXXX (name)
> Call them directly. I am not in between. There is no fee for you.
>
> Ask these when you visit:
> • How much deposit, and when do you get it back
> • Is the electricity bill separate
> • What time is the gate closed
> • Is mess food charged extra
>
> Tell me if you face any problem.

Four things that reply does: confirms the room is real, hands over the number
with no gatekeeping, states that the student pays nothing, and gives them the
questions they do not know to ask. That last part is what a senior would have
told them, and it is why they will remember you.

**Then label the chat `Number given`.**

**Before you send it, be sure the room is actually vacant.** A student sent to a
full PG will tell every friend in their batch. If you are not sure, say so and
check first — "let me confirm with the owner, 10 minutes" costs you nothing.

---

## 2. Tell the owner, same minute

> Hello sir, a student got your number from Aangan.
> Name: ___ , [college]. They will call you shortly.
>
> Is the room still empty? If not, tell me and I will remove it from the site.

Two jobs, one message. The owner learns a student came **from Aangan** — that
accumulating impression is the entire future pricing conversation, and it costs
you one forward. And you re-check the vacancy at the exact moment it matters.

If a particular owner is more comfortable in Marathi, send it in Marathi. Judge
that per person — student messages stay English.

---

## 3. Follow-up, 48 hours later

The step everyone skips, and the only way you ever learn whether any of this
works.

> What happened? Did you see the room? 🙂

Then label: `Visited`, `Moved in`, or `Did not go`.

If they did not go — **ask why.** Too expensive, too far, owner did not pick up,
found something else. Five of those answers tell you more about the Kolhapur
market than anything else you will collect this year.

If they did move in, ask one more:

> Great! One question — was everything on Aangan correct? Anything wrong or
> missing?

That is your listing-accuracy audit, free, from the only person who can do it.

---

## 4. Owner onboarding, after a meeting

Same evening, once the listing is entered:

> Sir, your PG is now on the site. Link: ___
>
> Please check it once — rent, deposit, food, is everything correct?
> If anything is wrong, tell me and I will change it immediately.
>
> If you ever want it removed, just call me. I will take it down, no questions.

Sending the link is what makes it real to him. The last line is the promise you
already make out loud — putting it in writing, in his own chat, is what makes it
believable.

---

## 5. Weekly vacancy check, during the waves

Every Monday, to every listed owner. Individually, not a broadcast.

> Hello sir. How many beds are empty today?

Short enough to answer in three words. Stale vacancy is what destroys a listing
site — one student sent to a full PG costs you more than five new listings gain.

Update `vacantBeds` the same day. If an owner stops answering twice, mark the
listing full rather than guessing.

---

## 6. The June list

Every student who did not convert — the ones already housed, the ones who found
something else, the ones from the KIT event:

> No problem! One request — can I keep your number? If you look for a room in
> June, I will tell you first.

Label `June list`. Message them in **April**, not June — by June they have
already asked a senior.

That list is worth more than anything else you build this year. Every name is a
student who will search again, already knows who you are, and cost you nothing.

---

## Rules that do not bend

**Never ask a student for money.** Not a booking fee, not a visit charge, not a
small amount for petrol. One message like that, screenshotted, ends this.

**Never guarantee safety.** `/verification` says you provide none. Do not
contradict your own site in a chat that can be screenshotted.

**Never give an owner's number before checking the room is vacant.**

**Answer within 30 minutes during the waves, or turn the button off.** A student
who waits four hours for a reply, instead of calling, is worse off for having
found you. If you cannot watch the inbox on a given day, point WhatsApp at the
owner for that day.

**Broadcast lists only reach people who have saved your number.** This catches
everyone out. For students, that is almost nobody — so send individually, or ask
them to save the number first.

---

## What to track, weekly

Five numbers, in the notebook, every Sunday:

1. Enquiries received
2. Numbers given out
3. Visits that happened
4. Students who moved in
5. Owners who confirmed vacancy this week

Number 4 is the business. Numbers 1 and 4 together give you a conversion rate,
and a conversion rate is what makes an owner pay you next year.
